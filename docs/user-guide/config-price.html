Prices

What a kilowatt-hour costs you and what you are paid for one — the two numbers every optimizer decision is weighed against.

What this is for

  • I am on a dynamic tariff and want the plan built on tomorrow's actual prices.
  • I have a fixed tariff and want my real rate used, surcharges included.
  • I am paid for what I export and want that weighed against self-consumption.

Everything here is price.*. Both the import price and the feed-in tariff are configured on this page, because the optimizer needs the pair to decide anything.

What you pay, and what you are paid

The import price can come from a market source, from a fixed rate, or from a 24-value array you supply; the adder and multiplier turn a raw market price into the figure on your bill — applied to every price source alike, as (price + adder) × (1 + multiplier), so a grid fee is multiplied the way the energy price is. The feed-in half is configured the same way, separately, because the two rarely come from the same place.

Danish feed-in prices (Elpris)

Set price.feed_in_source to elpris_dk and pick your bidding zone in price.feed_in_zone — DK1 west of the Great Belt, DK2 east of it, including Copenhagen. EOS Connect then reads the hourly day-ahead prices published by elprisenligenu.dk; no account or API key is needed.

Prices arrive one day per request, in euro and in kroner, with the exchange rate the provider used that day. Tomorrow’s file appears once the day-ahead auction clears, around 13:00 Danish time — before then the optimizer sees today repeated for the second half of its horizon, and the real curve replaces it at the next update. Updates run every 15 minutes; if the service is unreachable the last good prices are kept rather than dropped.

The static adder and multiplier apply on top in the feed-in convention ((price + adder) × multiplier) — use the adder for a fixed per-kWh deduction your buyer makes, and the multiplier for a percentage one. The negative-price switch clamps an hour to zero when the market price goes below it, which matches most Danish contracts.

Prices from your own source

With price.source set to timeseries, EOS Connect fetches the curve from an entity or URL you name, in the shared array format. The format, ready-made Home Assistant templates and a way to test it are under the timeseries guide.

Feed-in prices from your own source

The export half can come from a timeseries too: set price.feed_in_source to timeseries and point the price.feed_in_* connection fields at an entity or URL publishing your feed-in tariff — the same array format, field spellings, window rules and “Test connection” button as the import side, documented in the timeseries guide. The static adder and multiplier apply on top in the feed-in convention ((price + adder) × multiplier), the negative-price switch clamps below zero, and slots the series does not cover are filled by the usual policy and reported as forecast.

Smart price prediction

Day-ahead prices only reach as far as tomorrow evening, while the optimizer plans 48 hours. This fills the remainder with a predicted curve for your market zone, so the far end of the horizon is a forecast rather than a repeat of today.
